# Lesson 1: What Makes a Myth?  (type: Spark, [50, 50] min)

## SECTION 1: Launch  ([15, 15] min)
[PURPOSE]
  Students will build curiosity about myths by unpacking the word mythology and predicting what myths do for cultures.
[TABLE]
| H:**Teacher Tip** |
| Greek mythology began as an ancient religion centered on divine beings. Students may bring many different beliefs about god(s), religion, and spirituality to class. Frame the lesson by noting that students are studying myths as literature and cultural storytelling, not being asked to adopt any belief system. |
[/TABLE]
- **Say:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  We are launching a new unit that dives into myths and the rich and complex worlds they build. People in many cultures have used stories to explain mysteries, danger, and powerful forces they could not fully understand. By the end of this unit, you will compare myths and myth-based modern stories like }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _The Lightning Thief_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  to explain why these stories still matter.}
##### **Teach:**
- **Say:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  When I look at a multisyllabic word like }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _mythology,_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  I don’t try to understand it by tackling the entire word at once. Instead, I notice the base word }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _myth_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT: , meaning “a traditional story,” and the word part }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _‑logy,_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  meaning “study of.” Putting those pieces together helps me build the meaning: }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _mythology _}{TEACHER-SCRIPT: is “the study of traditional stories.” This matters because today we’re not just reading a single story; we’re beginning to think about an entire system of stories that reflect a culture’s beliefs and values.}
[ROUTINE icon=notes_section] Think-Pair-Share
_Have students open their __**Personal Dictionaries**__ and write mythology. Then have them circle the root myth and underline the suffix -logy before discussing with a partner._
**Say these Directions:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  Write mythology in your Personal Dictionary. Circle the part that names the story, underline the part that means “study of,” and then turn to a partner to build the word’s meaning together. Partner A shares first, then Partner B adds on.}
- **Ask:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  What does the word }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _mythology_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  mean, and how is that different from just one }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _myth_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT: ?}
  - {SAMPLE-RESPONSE: Mythology means “the study of myths,” or the whole group of myths in a culture. A myth is one story, but mythology is the system or collection of those stories.}
- **Ask:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  Based on the word mythology, why might myths be important in a culture?}
  - {SAMPLE-RESPONSE: I predict myths help a culture explain important ideas because mythology sounds like a whole body of stories people study and pass down.}
#### **Connection to Today’s Learning:**
- **Say:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  Now that we have built the meaning of }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _mythology_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT: , we are ready to read an informational text to test our predictions and decide what qualities make a story a myth.}

## SECTION 2: Learning in Action  ([30, 30] min)
[PURPOSE]
  Students will read What Are Myths? and identify details from the text that show how myths explain the world, reflect beliefs and values, and include supernatural elements. (RI.6.1, RI.6.4, SL.6.1.a, L.6.4.b)
- **Say:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  In this lesson, we are reading like investigators. As we read }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _What Are Myths?_}{TEACHER-SCRIPT: , we are looking for repeated ideas that help us understand what myths do.}
##### **Teach:**
- **Say:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  Strong readers don’t stop at a simple definition like “a myth is an old story.” Instead, they look for patterns across the text. When the article repeats ideas, like explaining the world, showing what people believe, or including gods and supernatural forces, those patterns help us build a clearer understanding of myths.}
[ROUTINE icon=notes_section] Whole Class Discussion
_Students should read selected sections of the article first, then discuss evidence with partners before sharing with others._
**Say these Directions:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  As you read, find details from the article that fit each category and record them in the }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: **3-Column Chart**}{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  graphic organizer.}
_Label each column in the chart as indicated:_
- Column 1: Explains the world
- Column 2: Reflects beliefs and values
- Column 3: Includes supernatural elements
_**Completed Sample Table for Teacher Reference**_
[TABLE]
| H:**Explains the world** | H:**Reflects beliefs and values** | H:**Includes supernatural elements** |
| myths explain floods, seasons, volcanoes | myths are sacred and guide how people live | gods, divine rulers, underworld |
| stories answer big questions like how the world began | tied to religion and cultural practices like Diwali | interactions between humans and gods |
| explain natural events people couldn’t scientifically understand | passed down to share beliefs across generations | magical beings and forces beyond humans |
[/TABLE]
[TABLE]
| H:[ROUTINE icon=cfu] Pulse Check (RI.6.1, RI.6.4) |
| Which statement best matches the definition of myth students built from today’s reading? / - A myth is only an exciting made-up story created to entertain readers. / - Incorrect: This choice reflects the misconception that myths are just fiction for entertainment and ignores explanation, values, and the supernatural. / - A myth is a traditional story that helps explain the world, reflects cultural values, and often includes supernatural elements. / - **Correct: This answer includes all three ideas students identified in the article.** / - A myth is a true biography about a famous ancient person. / - Incorrect: This response confuses myth with historical biography and ignores the role of supernatural forces and cultural explanation. / - A myth is a scientific text that proves how natural events happen. / - Incorrect: This answer confuses myth with science. Myths explain phenomena through story, not scientific proof. |
[/TABLE]
[COLLAPSIBLE "Integrated ELD Instructions"]
  [CALLOUT variant=purpose]
    Support students in using evidence-based language to explain how specific details from a text show what myths do.
    **Language Focus:**
    subordinating connector because
    academic verb shift from shows to reveals
    explanation frames for connecting evidence to categories
  [CALLOUT variant=say]
    Have students orally rehearse one frame: This detail fits this category because ___.
    Have students practice replacing shows with reveals in one sentence: One detail that reveals this is ___.
    Prompt students to explain why a detail belongs in a specific column, not just place it there.
    When students say shows, model when reveals is stronger because it signals reasoning about how the detail fits the category.
    You said “This goes in explains the world” — we can explain that by saying “This detail reveals that myths explain the world because it describes how people understood floods.”
    You said “This is about beliefs” — we can say “This detail reveals cultural beliefs because it shows how people used myths to guide how they live.”
    This detail fits ___ because ___.
    One detail that reveals this is ___.
    This belongs in ___ because it explains ___.
    Invite students to briefly connect the idea of myths to stories or traditions they know, if they choose, and then return to the text to find a matching detail.
    Allow partners to rehearse responses in a shared home language before sharing in English.
  [CALLOUT variant=watch]
    If students place a detail without explanation → Prompt: “Why does this detail fit this column? Start with ‘This detail fits ___ because…’”
    If students summarize instead of connecting to the category → Prompt: “How does this detail show what myths do? Add because and explain.”
    Student uses because to explain why a detail fits a category.
    Student uses reveals to connect a detail to a broader idea about myths.
[/COLLAPSIBLE]

## SECTION 3: Look Back  ([5, 5] min)
[PURPOSE]
  Students will reflect on how they figured out a working definition of myth and write an evidence-based response.
[ROUTINE icon=writing] Quick Write
- **Ask:**{TEACHER-SCRIPT:  What is a myth, and what in today’s text helped you figure that out? Write 3–4 sentences and include at least two specific details from }{TEACHER-SCRIPT: _What Are Myths?_}
  - {SAMPLE-RESPONSE: A myth is a traditional story that explains important ideas about the world and a culture. I figured this out from the part of the article that explains how myths helped people understand natural events they could observe but not explain. Another detail that reveals this is the part saying myths teach values, which means they pass beliefs from one generation to the next.}
